## **Where customer service costs actually come from**

Before you can cut costs, you need to see where they go. For a typical Brazilian SME, customer service spending breaks down into a few big buckets:

**Agent salaries and time.** The single largest cost. Every hour an agent spends answering messages is money — and most of those hours go to repetitive, low-complexity questions.

**Peak-season overload.** During Black Friday, Natal, and other peaks, volume can triple. Businesses either hire temporary staff (expensive) or let service quality collapse (costly in lost sales).

**Slow response \= lost sales.** Every unanswered message during busy hours or overnight is potential revenue walking away from a hidden cost that rarely shows up on a spreadsheet but hits the bottom line hard.

**After-hours gaps.** Customers message at night and on weekends. Without automation, those conversations wait and many don't come back.

The common thread: most of these costs come from *humans doing repetitive work* and from *not being able to respond fast enough at scale*. That's exactly what an AI chatbot addresses.

## **The math: how AI chatbots get you toward 60%**

Here's the honest reasoning behind the number. It comes down to one key fact about customer service: **the majority of incoming messages are repetitive.**

Across most Brazilian e-commerce and service operations, a large share of customer questions are the same handful of things: "where's my order?", "do you have this in stock?", "what are the payment options?", "what's the shipping time?", "how do I return this?". Industry-wide, these repetitive queries commonly make up 50–70% of total volume.

Now apply that to your own numbers. Let's use a simple example:

* Say your team handles **1,000 customer conversations a month.**  
* Suppose **60% of them are repetitive** questions a bot can fully resolve that's **600 conversations.**  
* An AI chatbot handles those 600 automatically, instantly, 24/7, at effectively no per-conversation labor cost.  
* Your human team now focuses only on the **400 conversations** that genuinely need a person.

In that scenario, you've removed roughly 60% of the *human workload* — which is where the "up to 60% cost reduction" comes from. The exact figure depends on how repetitive your specific volume is: if 70% of your messages are routine, your potential saving is higher; if only 40% are, it's lower. That's why the honest framing is *"up to"* — and why you should run this calculation on your own real numbers.

**Plug in your numbers:**

1. How many conversations do you handle per month?  
2. What percentage are repetitive questions a bot could answer?  
3. That percentage is roughly the share of workload an AI chatbot can lift off your team.

The result is usually eye-opening — and it's why automation isn't a cost, it's a cost *reduction*.

## **Doing it right: automate without hurting experience**

The wrong way to cut costs is to frustrate customers with a bot that traps them. The goal is to automate repetitive work while *improving* experience. A few principles make that happen:

**Automate the predictable, humanize the complex.** Let the AI handle the routine 60%. Route the genuinely complex or sensitive conversations to a human — smoothly, with full context, so the customer never repeats themselves.

**Use AI that understands real language.** Customers don't type in neat commands. They write the way people actually message — informally, with typos and abbreviations. Modern AI needs to understand that. Hello pairs a no-code chatbot builder with an agentic (instruction-based) AI that handles the unpredictable ways customers really phrase things, rather than breaking the moment someone goes off-script.

**Always offer a way to a person.** A clear, easy path to a human agent is essential. Automation should feel like faster service, never a wall.

**Keep improving with real data.** The best automations are refined over time based on the actual conversations customers have — closing gaps and handling more over time.

## **A realistic rollout plan**

You don't have to automate everything at once. A practical path to the savings:

1. **Identify your top repetitive questions.** Look at a week of conversations and list the most common ones. These are your automation targets.  
2. **Build flows for those first.** Start with the highest-volume questions — order status, availability, payment, shipping. This is where most of the 60% lives.  
3. **Add a smooth human handoff.** Make sure anything the bot can't resolve reaches a person cleanly.  
4. **Measure and expand.** Track how many conversations the bot resolves on its own, and keep adding flows for the next most common questions.

Within a short time, most businesses find the bot is handling the bulk of routine volume — and the cost savings follow.
