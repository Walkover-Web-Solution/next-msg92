# **Hello vs. Blip: Comparing Two Leading Customer Engagement Platforms (2026)**

*July 2026 · 12 min read*

If you're choosing a customer service and sales platform for your Brazilian business, Hello by MSG91 and Blip (Take Blip) are two names worth comparing. Both help you manage customer conversations, automate support, and sell more through WhatsApp and other channels — but they're built with different strengths and different types of business in mind.

This is an honest, fact-based comparison focused on features, channels, AI, and how each platform is built to work. Everything here comes from each platform's official pages. We'll look at what each does well, where they differ, and who each one fits best — so you can make a confident choice.

*(Note: Blip does not publish standard pricing openly for its main products, so this comparison focuses on capabilities rather than cost. For Blip pricing, you'd need to contact them directly.)*

---

## **First, an important clarification: Blip has two products**

Before comparing, it's worth knowing that Blip offers two distinct products, and they're built for different needs:

**Blip Go** is Blip's product for small businesses. Per Blip's own site, it's focused on sales and customer support on WhatsApp — it's designed around WhatsApp as the primary channel, and Blip's page lists needs like channels beyond WhatsApp and complex enterprise integrations under what it's "not recommended for." In other words, Blip Go is a focused, WhatsApp-centered tool for smaller operations.

**Blip Platform** (the full "Intelligent Contact" platform) is Blip's enterprise-grade product built for organizations operating at scale. This is the one that supports a wide channel list and deep automation — WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook Messenger, Blip Chat, Apple Messages for Business, SMS, email, Telegram, Microsoft Teams, and Google RCS — along with developer SDKs, an AI agent studio, and native NLP.

This matters because Hello compares differently to each. Below, we cover both.

---

## **Meet the platforms**

[**Hello by MSG91**](https://msg91.com/br/hello) is an all-in-one customer engagement platform. It brings together an omnichannel inbox spanning six messaging channels — WhatsApp, Instagram, email, live chat, Facebook Messenger, and RCS — along with a no-code chatbot builder, an agentic (instruction-based) AI, and built-in ticketing. It also offers voice bots for WhatsApp calling and phone IVR. As a Meta tech partner, MSG91 provides the official WhatsApp Business API, and Hello is designed to be self-serve: you can start free and go live in a day, with enterprise-grade security built in (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT).

**Blip (Take Blip)** is a well-established Brazilian conversational platform and official Meta partner. Through Blip Go it serves small businesses on WhatsApp, and through the Blip Platform it serves enterprise organizations with multichannel, highly customizable conversational automation.

---

## **Hello vs. Blip Go (the WhatsApp-focused product)**

If you're comparing Hello with Blip Go, the key difference is scope.

Blip Go is a focused, WhatsApp-centered tool for small businesses — a clean way to handle sales and support on WhatsApp. If WhatsApp is your main channel, it does that job well, backed by Blip's official Meta partnership.

Hello covers WhatsApp too, with the [official WhatsApp Business API](https://msg91.com/br/whatsapp) through Meta tech partner MSG91 and in the same account, it also brings five more channels (Instagram, email, live chat, Facebook Messenger, RCS), plus built-in ticketing, voice bots, and an agentic AI. So if you want WhatsApp today with the option to add other channels and capabilities as you grow without moving to a separate, heavier product later, Hello gives you that room in one place.

## **Hello vs. Blip Platform (the enterprise product)**

If you're comparing Hello with the full Blip Platform, this is more of an apples-to-apples multichannel comparison — both are broad, capable platforms.

**Blip Platform focuses on conversational automation at scale.** Its strength is deep chatbot building and orchestration a visual Builder plus developer SDKs (C\#, JavaScript), native NLP through BLU, and an AI agent Studio where you configure LLMs, knowledge bases, and guardrails. It supports a very wide channel list (WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook Messenger, Blip Chat, Apple Messages for Business, SMS, email, Telegram, Microsoft Teams, Google RCS). This depth is powerful, though building sophisticated flows and agents can involve technical resources or developer support to get the most out of it.

**Hello focuses on all-in-one capability that a business team can run itself.** It combines the omnichannel inbox, a no-code drag-and-drop chatbot builder, an agentic instruction-based AI, ticketing, and voice bots in one platform with unlimited agents on every plan and enterprise-grade security (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT). The emphasis is on getting a capable operation running quickly and self-serve, so a non-technical team can build and manage automation without developers, while still having ticketing and voice automation bundled in.

## **AI and automation**

Both platforms invest heavily in AI, but approach it differently.

The Blip Platform offers a robust AI stack: a Studio for building and orchestrating AI agents (configuring LLMs, connecting knowledge bases, defining instructions and guardrails), its native BLU natural-language understanding, and a Copilot that assists human agents. This is a deep, flexible toolset well suited to teams that want to engineer sophisticated conversational systems and have the resources to do so.

Hello pairs a no-code drag-and-drop builder with an agentic, instruction-based AI that works from your instructions to handle real customer conversations. The focus is on giving a business team powerful automation they can set up and adjust themselves, without a developer, combined with built-in ticketing and voice bots in the same platform.

The practical difference: Blip Platform offers greater depth for teams that want to engineer complex agents; Hello offers faster, self-serve automation that a non-technical team can own end to end.

---

## **Ease of setup and use**

Hello is fully self-serve: you connect your channels, build flows with the no-code builder, and can go live in about a day, with onboarding help articles available in Portuguese — no sales process required to start.

Blip Go is also self-serve and works with your existing WhatsApp number. The Blip Platform, being a deep enterprise product, typically involves a more guided or technical setup to make full use of its Builder, SDKs, and AI Studio. For organizations with technical resources, that depth is an asset; for a lean team that wants to launch quickly on its own across multiple channels, Hello's self-serve model is faster to value.

---

## **Who each option is best for**

**Blip Go is a strong fit if:**

* WhatsApp is your primary channel and you want a focused tool for it  
* You're a small business wanting simple WhatsApp sales and support

**Blip Platform is a strong fit if:**

* You want to engineer sophisticated, custom conversational systems at scale  
* You have developer resources to leverage its SDKs and AI Studio  
* You need a very wide channel list including Telegram, Microsoft Teams, and Apple Messages

**Hello is a strong fit if:**

* You want an all-in-one platform (inbox, chatbot, agentic AI, ticketing, voice bots) in one place  
* You want WhatsApp plus five more channels without moving to a separate product as you grow  
* You want unlimited agents on every plan, with no per-seat charges  
* You prefer self-serve setup and going live quickly, without a technical project  
* You want a non-technical team to build and manage automation themselves  
* You want enterprise-grade security (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT) built in
