# **Hello vs. Zenvia: Which Customer Service Platform Fits Your Business? (2026)**

*July 2026 · 11 min read*

If you're choosing a customer service and sales platform for your Brazilian business, Hello by MSG91 and Zenvia are two names worth comparing. Both help you manage customer conversations, automate support, and sell more but they're built with different strengths and different types of business in mind.

This is an honest, fact-based comparison. Every feature and price here comes from each platform's official pages. We'll look at what each does well, where they differ, and who each fits best so you can make a confident choice.

---

## **Meet the two platforms**

**Zenvia** is one of the largest and most established communication companies in Latin America, publicly listed and headquartered in São Paulo. Its Zenvia Customer Cloud is a broad platform spanning mass messaging, multichannel customer service, generative AI, chatbots, and data governance. It supports seven channels (SMS, WhatsApp, RCS, email, Facebook Messenger, Instagram, and Webchat) through channel packages, priced as a software plan plus a separate channel package.

[**Hello by MSG91**](https://msg91.com/br/hello) is an all-in-one, WhatsApp-first customer engagement platform. It brings together an omnichannel inbox spanning six messaging channels WhatsApp, Instagram, email, live chat, Facebook Messenger, and RCS along with a no-code chatbot builder, an agentic (instruction-based) AI, and built-in ticketing. It also offers voice bots for WhatsApp calling and phone IVR. As a Meta tech partner, MSG91 provides the official [WhatsApp Business API](https://msg91.com/br/whatsapp), and Hello is designed to be self-serve: you can start free and go live in a day.

Both are capable. The right choice depends on your size, your priorities, and how you like to buy and run software.

---

## **Features: where they overlap and where they differ**

On the fundamentals, these platforms have a lot in common. Both centralize multiple channels, both offer the official WhatsApp API, both include AI, both provide chatbots and automation, and both offer ticketing and mass messaging.

The differences show up in scale, structure, and how you buy.

**Zenvia offers breadth across channels.** It's a wide platform with generative AI, data governance, SLA-based support, configurable automations, and a channel catalog sold as separate packages. Its structure a software plan plus individually-priced channel packages plus per-user fees gives large operations flexibility, though it means more moving parts to assemble and manage.

[**Hello**](https://msg91.com/br/hello) **delivers comparable capability in one bundled, all-in-one platform.** It combines the omnichannel inbox, no-code chatbot builder plus agentic AI, ticketing, and voice bots together  with unlimited agents on every plan and enterprise-grade security built in (ISO 27001 certified, SOC 2 Type II audited, and VAPT tested). Instead of assembling a software plan plus separate channel packages plus per-user fees, Hello keeps everything in straightforward plans that scale from a free tier up serving small teams and larger operations alike, without per-seat costs or a layered setup.

## **Ease of setup and use**

Both platforms are approachable in their own way, but they're built for different buying styles.

Hello is fully self-serve: you connect your channels, build your flows with the no-code builder, and can go live in about a day, with onboarding help articles available in Portuguese no setup fee and no sales call required.

Zenvia's entry Starter plan is self-serve too, but its model is more layered: you choose a software plan, then add a channel package, and enabling WhatsApp and/or RCS carries a one-time setup fee in the first month (ranging from R$649 on lower plans up to R$3.999 on Professional). For larger plans and the Enterprise tier, you work with sales. This structure gives large operations flexibility, but it's more to configure than a single bundled plan.

## **Who each platform is best for**

**Zenvia is a strong fit if:**

* You want a broad channel catalog (seven channels) with flexible per-channel packages  
* You specifically prefer a layered software-plus-channel-package purchasing model  
* You want SMS as a primary high-volume channel with tiered per-message rates  
* Your procurement is set up for per-user licensing and separate channel contracts

**Hello is a strong fit if:**

* You want to start free and scale cost only as you grow  
* You want unlimited users on every plan, with no per-seat charges  
* You prefer simple, bundled pricing with no separate channel package or setup fee  
* You want enterprise-grade security (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT) without enterprise complexity  
* You want self-serve setup and going live quickly  
* You want ticketing, chatbot, agentic AI, and voice bots bundled together in one platform
