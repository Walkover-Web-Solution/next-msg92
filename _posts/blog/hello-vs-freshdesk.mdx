# **Hello vs. Freshdesk: One Bundled Plan vs. Per-Agent Tiers and Add-Ons — The 2026 Comparison for Brazilian Businesses.** 

*July 2026 · 12 min read*

If you're choosing a customer service platform for your Brazilian business, Hello by MSG91 and Freshdesk are two names worth comparing. Freshdesk, from Freshworks, is a well-known, easy-to-use help desk used by tens of thousands of companies worldwide; Hello is an all-in-one, WhatsApp-first customer engagement platform. Both can run your support operation — but they're built on different models, and that difference shows up most clearly in how they package features and charge for them.

This is an honest, fact-based comparison. Freshdesk's pricing here comes from Freshworks' official pricing pages (verified mid-2026); Hello's comes from its own pricing page. We'll look at what each does well, where they differ, and who each one fits best — so you can decide with evidence.

---

## **Meet the two platforms**

**Freshdesk** is a customer support platform from Freshworks, known for being approachable and easy to set up. It's used by tens of thousands of businesses, including large brands. Importantly, Freshdesk sells under **two separate product lines**: **Freshdesk Support Desk** (the classic, ticketing-first help desk — email, chat, phone, and social ticketing) and **Freshdesk Omni** (the broader omnichannel suite that bundles ticketing with chat, a bot, and messaging channels like WhatsApp). They have separate price sheets, which matters a lot when you compare.

[**Hello by MSG91**](https://msg91.com/br/hello) is an all-in-one customer engagement platform. It brings together an omnichannel inbox across six messaging channels WhatsApp, Instagram, email, live chat, Facebook Messenger, and RCS plus a no-code chatbot builder, an agentic (instruction-based) AI, and built-in ticketing. It also offers voice bots for WhatsApp calling and phone IVR. As a Meta tech partner, MSG91 provides the [official WhatsApp Business API](https://msg91.com/br/whatsapp), and Hello is self-serve: you can start free and go live in a day, backed by enterprise-grade security (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT).

Both are capable and both are known for being easy to adopt. The right choice depends on your size, your channel needs, and how you want to be billed.

---

## **The biggest difference: how you're charged**

This is the clearest, most verifiable difference between the two.

**Freshdesk charges per agent, per month.** Every person who works in the system is another recurring seat cost. Your bill grows with every agent you add — not just with your ticket volume.

**Hello charges by usage and tickets, with unlimited agents.** Every Hello plan — including the free tiers — includes unlimited agents and unlimited teams. Adding people doesn't raise your plan price. You pay for usage and tickets, not headcount.

There's a second layer to understand with Freshdesk: because it splits into Support Desk and Omni, the plan you *think* you're buying (the cheaper ticketing line) may not be the one you actually need (the pricier omnichannel line) once you want WhatsApp and chat. With Hello, all six channels are included in the standard plans — there's no separate omnichannel tier to upgrade to.

### **What this means in practice**

The models are structurally different, so compare by team size and channels. If you need WhatsApp and chat on Freshdesk, you're likely on the Omni line so a 10-person team starts at $290/month (Omni Growth) and rises to $790/month (Omni Pro), before AI add-ons. On Hello, that same 10-person team is on the same $40 or $80 plan as a 1-person team, because agents are unlimited and all channels are included.

Two things widen the gap as you grow: Freshdesk's per-agent model (every new hire adds cost) and its AI pricing (Copilot per agent, plus per-session bot charges). Hello includes AI chatbots with no per-agent or per-session AI fee, and its free plan has no time limit.

## **Features: where they overlap and where they differ**

Both platforms cover the fundamentals: ticketing, omnichannel support, chatbots, AI, automation, and integrations. The differences are in packaging.

**Freshdesk's strengths.** It's genuinely easy to use and quick to set up — one of its most praised qualities. It has a mature ticketing core, hundreds of marketplace integrations, strong reporting, and solid enterprise features (audit logs, IP whitelisting, skill-based routing) at the Enterprise tier. For a team that wants a classic, approachable help desk, it's a strong, established option.

[**Hello's**](https://msg91.com/br/hello) **strengths.** It bundles more into the base offering: all six channels in the standard plans (no separate omnichannel tier), a no-code chatbot builder plus agentic AI with no per-agent or per-session AI fee, built-in ticketing, voice bots for WhatsApp calling and IVR, and real-time translation — all with unlimited agents. It's self-serve with no implementation fees, designed to go live in a day.

A key packaging difference: on Freshdesk, both AI (Copilot per agent \+ AI Agent per session) and omnichannel (the Omni line) cost more on top of the base ticketing price. Hello includes AI chatbots and all channels in its standard plans. For a business that wants AI and multichannel as standard rather than as stacked add-ons, that's a meaningful distinction.

## **Ease of setup and use**

Ease of use is one of Freshdesk's most recognized strengths; it's widely regarded as approachable and quicker to set up than heavier enterprise platforms, though its more advanced features can require additional training, and larger deployments may involve implementation services.

Hello is designed to be self-serve from the start: connect your channels, build flows with the no-code builder, and go live in about a day, with onboarding help articles in Portuguese and no implementation or integration fees. For a Brazilian SME that wants to be running quickly without a project, that's a real advantage.

## **Who each platform is best for**

**Freshdesk is a strong fit if:**

* You want a classic, well-established, easy-to-use help desk  
* Your support is primarily ticketing-first (email-centric), where the Support Desk line fits  
* You want a large marketplace of integrations  
* You're comfortable with a per-agent model and adding AI/omnichannel as paid layers

**Hello is a strong fit if:**

* You want unlimited agents without per-seat costs that grow with your team  
* You want all channels (including WhatsApp) included, not in a separate pricier tier  
* You want AI chatbots included, without per-agent or per-session AI fees  
* You want a free plan with no time limit  
* You want self-serve setup, live in a day, with no implementation fee  
* You want enterprise-grade security (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, VAPT)

---

## **The bottom line**

Freshdesk is a mature, approachable, well-established help desk with genuine strengths — ease of use, a large integration marketplace, and solid enterprise features. Its model is per-agent, split across a ticketing line and a pricier omnichannel line, with AI sold as add-ons (Copilot per agent and the AI bot per session) so the real cost depends heavily on your channels, team size, and AI usage.

Hello takes a different approach suited to growing Brazilian businesses: unlimited agents on every plan, all six channels included in the standard plans, AI chatbots included without per-agent or per-session fees, a free plan with no time limit, and self-serve setup you can launch in a day all backed by enterprise-grade security. It delivers the core capabilities a business needs without per-seat costs, channel-tier upgrades, or stacked AI charges.
